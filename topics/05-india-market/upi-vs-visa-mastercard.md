# Why Visa and Mastercard face a challenge in India

## TL;DR
India's **Unified Payments Interface (UPI)** processes **13bn+ real-time transactions a month** and is central to the digital economy – but Visa and Mastercard aren't in the loop.

## What's happening
- Digital payments rose after the **2016 demonetisation**; UPI enabled instant bank transfers via mobile, QR codes and phone numbers.
- UPI = ~**70%** of all transactions and **36%** of consumer spending (Bernstein).
- Credit cards' share of digital payments fell from **43% (2018) → 21% (2024)**.
- 2022: government gave Indian network **RuPay** exclusive rights to process credit-card transactions via UPI.
  - RuPay almost doubled transactions and nearly tripled its value share of credit-card sales to **28%**.
- Pricing advantage: RuPay-on-UPI only charges merchants on transactions above **₹2,000 (~$23.30)** → attractive to small businesses.
- RuPay = **half of all new credit cards** issued in June 2024.
- Bernstein: if UPI linkage stays RuPay-exclusive, RuPay likely becomes the dominant credit-card network.

## Why UPI matters
- Merchant fees from Visa/Mastercard are a global grievance usually tackled by regulators; India's approach is different – and could be replicated by countries wanting less dependence on Western networks.
- Visa/Mastercard are partnering with fintechs to support UPI-powered terminals in small shops – uncertain if enough.
- **Existential risk**: QR-based payments could dominate credit too, with bank credit accounts linked directly to UPI, **bypassing cards**.
