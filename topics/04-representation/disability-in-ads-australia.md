# No downside to featuring disabled people in ads

## TL;DR
Over **20%** of Australians have a disability, but **<1%** of ads feature a disabled person. An "audacious experiment" re-cast real ads with disabled people and found they perform **just as well** – and build stronger connection with disabled audiences.

## The experiment
- Partners: Luma Research, creative agency **Special Group**, and **Shift 20** (nonprofit founded by disabled athlete Dylan Alcott).
- **12 ads** already on air were recast and reshot to include a person with a disability – sometimes a minor character, sometimes the lead.
- Example: **Bonds** (underwear) swapped its lead for a deaf actor; the actor narrated using closed captions and sign language – message and story unchanged.
- Others included McDonald's, Uber and Kia.

## Method
- Panel of **1,800** Australians (25% with a disability).
- Original vs revised ads tested on four criteria:
  - **Attention** (is it engaging?)
  - **Brand link** (is it visible and clear?)
  - **Takeout** (impact on brand perception)
  - **Bonding** (feelings toward brand, purchase intent)

## Results
- Ads featuring disabled people are **just as effective** as those that don't.
- Casting resonates the same way regardless of whether the actor is disabled.
- Equally likely to get attention, build engagement and leave positive brand feelings.
- Disabled people are **more likely to consider** brands that cast someone with a disability, and feel more valued.
- **70%** said it's important for brands to take a stand on social issues.

## Takeaways for marketers
- Inclusion carries no effectiveness penalty – and gains for an under-served 20% of the market.
- Advertising can be an agent of social change, not only a target of criticism (Phillips, paraphrased).
