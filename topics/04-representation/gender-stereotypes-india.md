# Gender stereotypes persist in India's advertising

## TL;DR
A decade after Ariel's landmark **"Share the Load"** campaign on unequal household work, gender stereotypes remain common in Indian ads.

## What's the problem
- Brands address gender inequality at **board level**, but their marketing still perpetuates old stereotypes.
- A disconnect between stated intentions and content; traditional approaches struggle against entrenched mindsets.
- Even "empowered" portrayals can be clichés shaped by a male lens (Sujala Martis, Platinum Guild International).
- The "woman who has it all" (career, family, perfect home) isn't breaking a stereotype – it sets **unrealistic expectations**.
- Portrayals of **men and fatherhood** have yet to catch up with reality.

## Why representation matters
- Ariel reported **sales doubled** when "Share the Load" first ran.
- UN **Unstereotype Alliance** study – thousands of campaigns, four years, **58 markets** – found better gender representation can deliver significantly improved brand and financial outcomes.
- Brands that go beyond "performative activism" can reap the benefits.

## Key idea (paraphrased)
Consumer aspirations evolve faster than many brands realise; those who lead the change earn deeper trust and relevance (Martis).

## Takeaways for marketers
- Audit creative for subtle clichés, not just overt stereotypes.
- Modernise depictions of men as well as women.
