# Goodles – the "maximalist" approach to brand building

## TL;DR
Mac-and-cheese challenger Goodles (founded 2020) rejected minimalist "blanding" and went maximalist. It sells **one box every second** in the US.

## Key facts
- ~3.5 years in mainstream retail.
- **$4bn** annual revenue *in its category* (as reported), **23 products**, **175,000 distribution points**.
- Team of "big corporate CPG dropouts" set out to unlearn big-company conventions.
- Went big early: hired the best rather than running lean.

## The rules Goodles swears by
| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Be known for colour, not *a* colour | Three striking colours combined in interesting ways → shelf impact. Packaging is the most important advertising. |
| What's in a name? | No descriptive/boring names: *Ched Over Heels*, *Shell We Dance* |
| Maximum viable product | 1,000+ recipe iterations → protein, fibre, no artificial flavours. Relentless product development. |
| Be the life of the party | No traditional advertising; memorable activations (5k race, prom-style dance party with colander light fixtures) |
| Brand on parade, everywhere | Even buyer meetings: placemats, music, cooking, rollerskating – no PowerPoint |
| Build fans, not targets | Drop "user" and "target" from the lexicon |
| Manage your brand as if it's your last | Go all in – they bought and painted a dune buggy because it felt on brand |

## Takeaways for marketers
- In crowded categories, distinctiveness beats safety.
- Product quality is the foundation for the bold brand layer.
- Brand codes (colour, naming, tone) should show up at every touchpoint, including B2B.
