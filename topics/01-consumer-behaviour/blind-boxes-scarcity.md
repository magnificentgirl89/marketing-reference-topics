# Blind boxes – mystery plus scarcity drives purchase

## TL;DR
A China-based study (Elsevier) shows that combining **mystery** (you don't know which item you'll get) with **perceived scarcity** is effective at motivating purchase – but the *type* of scarcity should match the *type* of product.

## Core concept: hedonic vs utilitarian
| Hedonic products | Utilitarian products |
|---|---|
| Aesthetic pleasure, emotional and sensory experience | Functional purpose, rational needs |
| e.g. Labubu dolls, collectibles | e.g. everyday goods |

Two types of scarcity: **supply-based** (producer limits availability) and **demand-based** (demand outstrips supply).

## Findings
- **Supply scarcity + hedonic product** → triggers feelings of loss of control and emotional arousal.
- **Demand scarcity + utilitarian product** → sense of urgency, more impulse purchases.
- Limiting supply works better for hedonic products than utilitarian ones.
- Outcomes are linked to underlying neuropsychological mechanisms.

## Key facts
- Pop Mart sales grew consistently 2019–2023.
- H1 2024: **4.56bn yuan** in sales, **+62% YoY**.

## Takeaways for marketers
- Match scarcity tactic to product goal: "limited edition / drops" for emotional products; "selling fast / high demand" cues for functional ones.
- Use ethically – manufactured scarcity can backfire if it feels manipulative.
