# Shrinkflation bites in the UK

## TL;DR
Manufacturers are cutting pack sizes while holding or raising prices. It risks charges of profiteering and reputational damage. Retailers can turn it into an advantage by calling it out.

## Key facts (Which?)
- Sainsbury's Scottish Oats: pack halved, price up 68% → **236% rise per 100g**.
- Aquafresh Complete Care toothpaste: pack shrank by a quarter, price up 54% → **105% rise per 100ml**.
- Most increases are smaller (multipacks shrink, a few grams cut) but still add up.
- UK grocery inflation: **5.2%** in the year to August 2025.

## Skimpflation
- Confectionery hit by rising raw-material (cocoa) costs → recipes reformulated.
- Penguin and Club now contain more palm/shea oil than cocoa → can only be called **"chocolate-flavoured"**, not chocolate biscuits. Club even changed its advertising slogan to lean on "biscuit" rather than "chocolate".

## What's the answer?
- Brands find it hard to respond – they're not on the front line.
- **Intermarché (France)** flagged brands practising shrinkflation directly on shelves, blaming manufacturers rather than the retailer.
  - Result: own-label sales **+5.3%**, with no media costs.

## Takeaways for marketers
- Shrinkflation erodes trust – see "enshittification" in the [Sephora note](../02-brand-strategy/sephora-customer-experience.md).
- Transparency can be a competitive weapon for retailers and own-label.
